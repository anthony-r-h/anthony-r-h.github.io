# Introduction

The gunboat is a particularly American take on the dress shoe. British country brogues share some of its bulk, while Italian dress shoes often favour a lighter, slimmer shape. The American longwing went heavy, with broad uppers and thick leather soles. In postwar America, makers like Alden, Florsheim and Allen Edmonds helped make this substantial version of the country brogue a staple of business dress, worn with sack suits and button-down shirts.

The renewed interest in American menswear during the 2000s brought those shoes back into view, alongside workwear, Ivy clothes and midcentury tailoring. Thom Browne made the slim grey suit and heavy wingtip his signature. The short, close-fitting jacket and narrow trousers cropped above the ankle gave the heavy wingtip a different setting, leaving its broguing and thick sole edges exposed against an otherwise restrained suit. That pairing became one of the decade's defining menswear looks, helping push tailoring toward slimmer cuts and shorter hems well beyond his own label.

I've had my Allen Edmonds MacNeils for over ten years, and they've seen me through countless important events. I'd already had them resoled once, but the repair was poorly done and the shoe eventually came apart. I'd been wanting to get into shoemaking, and with plenty of leatherworking experience to draw on, I figured there was no harm in attempting the repair myself.

# Leatherwork and shoemaking

Shoemaking shares plenty of tools and techniques with other leatherwork, but some of the differences are substantial. My own experience so far is just pattern work, so cutting soles and lasting are still unknowns for me.
